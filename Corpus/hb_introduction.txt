Introduction

When you join the Beloit College community, it is assumed you are a responsible individual who
will grow and develop both academically and personally. To safeguard the rights, opportunities,
and welfare of students, faculty, staff and guests of the College, all students are held accountable
for the regulations and policies established by the College. Students are part of a community
where they are expected to be active and involved learners while living by the values of personal
integrity, responsibility, honesty, civility, and non-violence. Students are to have a respect for
the rights, opinions, and beliefs of others and have respect for all members of the college
community.

To carry out its educational mission and provide an atmosphere conducive to the building of
community and personal growth, the College requires a community free from physical and
emotional violence, threats, and intimidation, protective of free inquiry and expression, and
respectful of the rights of others. Community members need to be open to change, supportive of
democratic and lawful procedures, and dedicated to the rational and orderly approach to the
resolution of human problems. Personal idealism, social responsibility and service to others are
among the educational aims of Beloit College. Self-regulation is the norm, and College disciplinary
action the exception.

The Beloit College Student Handbook is your guide to your life as a part of the college community.
It is written and compiled by the Dean of Students staff and the Director of Security to inform
students of policies, procedures, and regulations that pertain to students. There are many pieces
of information that assist you, the student, in making responsible decisions and understanding the
College’s expectations and your own role and responsibilities as a member of the Beloit College
community.

Every student is responsible for knowing the information in the Handbook as well as the
information in the College catalog. Both are available online to be easily accessed, and all students,
particularly those who are new to Beloit, should read both thoroughly. The Handbook is updated
at least once per year, and students are expected to follow the most current edition. This
expectation includes commuter students as well as residential students, as there are policies and
regulations pertaining to ALL students.

Please Note: While this Handbook provides a review of regulations, policies, guidelines, and
resources, the College reserves the right to change statements and policies when necessary and
appropriate.

Questions about policies and regulations in the Student Handbook are to be directed to the Dean
of Students.

The following expectations, regulations and policies have been established as a guide to individual
conduct aimed at protecting the rights, opportunities, and welfare of students, faculty, staff and
guests of the College. These regulations do not attempt to include all possible scenarios for
unacceptable behaviors. Student enrollment, continued attendance, and access to college
resources at Beloit are a privilege, not a right. Revocation of this privilege occurs when it is
assessed that it is necessary to protect the campus community, is necessary for the safety of the
individual student, and/or the student is delinquent on payments to the College. In most cases,
sanctions are educational in nature and fulfill the College’s role in shaping character and the values
of personal integrity and responsibility, honesty, civility, non-violence, respect for the rights,
opinions and beliefs of others, and service to the college community and beyond. Students who
have ideological differences with College regulations and policies are encouraged to suggest
changes and revisions through the appropriate channels (Dean of Students) but are held
accountable for those contained herein.
