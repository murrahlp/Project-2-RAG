Gender Neutral/LBGTQ+IA Housing
Beloit’s strong tradition of empowering students with alternative gender and sexual identities has
been a hallmark of the College’s acceptance of diversity. In order to advance our proud history of
supporting all students, Gender Neutral Housing is available for a more comfortable and safe
on-campus environment for students with alternative gender and sexual identities by allowing
students to room with another individual regardless of sex or gender identity.

Students who want to live on a gender-neutral floor must sign the Statement of Intent before they may
choose a gender-neutral room, but they do not have to identify a specific reason for picking
gender-neutral housing. The process for selecting rooms will be the same as any other on campus:
students must participate in the lottery and may select a gender-neutral room when their number
comes up. All roommates must be chosen ahead of time (no “open-doubles'' to be filled by Residence
Life). If a roommate leaves the room, the current resident will be given an opportunity to find a
replacement. Should none be found, the room will revert to the legal sex of the resident and Residence
Life may put another student in the room. Additionally, with enough interest, the College maintains at
least one floor featuring LBGTQ+IA interests.

2. General Housing Expectations/Policies/Services
Beloit College recognizes that learning is not confined to the classroom but also encompasses a
broad range of co-curricular activities and experiences. One of the most important of these is the
interactions among students, faculty and staff which occur on a residential campus.

The residence halls are largely self-governing and operate on the basic principle of mutual respect and
consideration for the rights of all residents. Students are encouraged to accept individual and collective
responsibilities for setting their own guidelines in these areas. However, the College reserves the right
to change room assignments and occupancy levels in the interest of health, discipline, housing needs, or
the general welfare of the student and/or other students.

In order to ensure smooth functioning of the living areas (residence halls, special interest houses,
Greek houses and apartments), the protection of students’ individual rights, and the protection of
personal and College property, a number of operating policies and procedures are in effect in the living
areas:

Air Conditioning: Air conditioning units may not be installed in any residence space. The
following residential spaces have A/C (when Facilities turns it on): Aldrich, Moore Town Houses, the
Clary Apartments, Peet, Porter, Whitney, Blaisdell, Bushnell, Maurer, Brannon, and Chapin.

Alcohol: Please see the full policy (Section X, A 1). Students who are 21 and over may drink alcohol in
their rooms, as long as they are following the Alcohol Policy.

Bicycles: Bicycle storage space is very limited and only available in Whitney storage on a first-come,
first-serve basis. Students are not to keep bikes in common areas (i.e. attached to stairwells,

furnishings, etc.). Bikes left in these areas will have the locks cut off and the item removed. Students
may leave their bikes outside tethered to the bike racks. It is important to make sure that one’s bike is
locked to the rack. Students may not leave their bikes on campus after the academic year unless they
will be attending over the summer semester.. Unless specific arrangements are made with Residence
Life, bikes left unattended for more than one month following the end of the academic year are
considered abandoned, and may be confiscated, sold, donated, or destroyed without compensation.
Students are strongly encouraged to have a means to identify a bike as their own and register their
bikes with Security.

Bulletin Boards: Used as a prime method of communication, each resident is responsible for knowing
the contents of all official notices on bulletin boards or posting areas. Any graffiti that is posted or any
derogatory or discriminatory comments will be taken down, reported to Security, and the parties
involved will be met with by staff and could face potential adjudication.

Candles: Candles, kerosene lamps, incense or incense burners (see “Incense” below), or any other open
flame devices are not permitted in any of the living areas under any circumstances for fire safety
reasons.

Check-In/Out: Students may move into the residence halls at the date and time indicated on the
website and must be completely checked out of their assigned space by 5 p.m. the day after their last
final examination, but no later than published dates. Any students seeking an exemption must
submit a written request to the Residence Life Office two weeks prior to the end of the semester.

Doors: Suite, hallway, and room doors that are left propped open create a potential security breach and
fire hazard. For the sake of all residents, please leave all doors closed and locked (when appropriate).

Fire Alarms, Smoke Detectors, and Extinguishers: Fire safety equipment (fire extinguishers, fire
alarms, and smoke detectors) and evacuation/exit plans are installed in every building to protect the
residents in case of a fire. Tampering with this equipment endangers the safety of others, is a violation
of Wisconsin law, and will be considered a serious offense by the College. All tampering and/or damage
to fire equipment will result in a fine for the offender, or a fine per person on the floor where the
incident occurred if no specific person is found to be responsible.

Covering a smoke detector in order to render it not operational is a serious violation of policy. In
addition, disciplinary action and criminal action may be taken, including fines and increased room
inspections focusing on making sure that the smoke detectors have not been tampered with. Repeated
offenses may result in suspension or expulsion.

Should the student have a legitimate need to discharge an extinguisher, this action must be reported
immediately to the Security Office; if found that it was a legitimate need, no fines will be assessed. In all
cases, should the individual responsible for the tampering come forward, the fine is likely to be reduced
but at the minimum will be responsible for the cost of replacement along with possible disciplinary and

criminal action. In addition to this, there will be two inspections of smoke detectors per year. If a smoke
detector is not in place, in addition to fines, a replacement detector will be installed and the student will
be charged the cost of the detector and labor at time and one-half rates.

Immediately upon moving into college housing, each resident should become acquainted with the fire
exit routes, locations of extinguishers, alarms, and hall evacuation routes and procedures.

Fire Evacuation: When a fire alarm sounds, all residents are to evacuate the building according to the
following procedures:

1. Notify Security immediately at (608) 363-2355, while moving to exit the building.

2. Evacuation shall be by the nearest exit. Remain calm and exit in an orderly fashion.

3. Students are to move away from the building to a minimum of 300 feet and out of the way
of any emergency personnel. Each person in charge of a group (i.e. RA for their floor)
should make a head count to ensure, if possible, that all persons have evacuated the
building. All residents of a hall shall meet in the area designated on the hall evacuation plan
as the meeting place. If a person is thought to still be in the hall, notify the security officer
and they will immediately notify the fire department.

4. If you know that you caused the fire alarm, approach a security officer (once you have
evacuated) and indicate where/how you think you caused the fire alarm to sound. Students
are typically not fined if it appears accidental (i.e. spilled food on a hot burner in the kitchen;
set popcorn time too long in the microwave causing it to smoke, etc.), unless the behavior is
repeated or appears intentional.

5. DO NOT ENTER or RE-ENTER the building under any circumstances until the all clear has
been given. Security will transmit the all-clear information when advised to do so by
emergency personnel.

Students can be arrested for not evacuating when the fire alarm sounds, tampering with fire
extinguishers and smoke detectors, and, obviously, setting fires.

Fire Hazards - No Open Flames: Any open flames, including candles, kerosene lamps, incense or
incense burners, or any other open flame devices are not permitted in any campus buildings (including
residences). Some classrooms may have them under faculty supervision for educational purposes.

Fire Pit (Aldrich Field): Use of the Aldrich firepit is scheduled/approved through Security
(608-336-2355 or security@beloit.edu) - This is the only firepit allowed to be used on the residential side
of campus. Please reach out to the SEAL Director to ensure the supplies are well stocked.

Food Oversight Committee: This is the primary agent for feedback, evaluation, planning, and
communication regarding campus dining services. This committee needs student help and input, and,
therefore, invites students to share ideas and thoughts during regularly scheduled meetings. To apply
for membership with this committee, reach out to the Beloit Student Government or the SEAL Director.

Residential Experience Advisory Committee: REAC operates as a point of contact and
conversation between campus community members and the residential and student life staff as well as
other campus leadership, as needed. Points of conversation may include service offerings, spending
priorities, strategic direction, and compliance concerns. REAC represents various community members
by communicating with peers and highlighting ideas for improvements. When needed, REAC may
acquire relevant data, invite other administrators to participate, and identify resources to consider the
feasibility of improvements and assist in prioritizing the timeline for the proposed changes.

Furniture Moving: College property, including furnishings, screens/storms, must not be removed
from assigned areas without authorization from a professional Residence Life staff member (not peer
Resident Assistants). Students who remove furniture or other items from their designated areas will be
billed a per day fee and a per item fee for having the item returned to its original location. A student
whose room is missing the original furnishings upon check out will be assessed a charge for the
replacement value of these items.

Halogen Lamps: New halogen lamps with lamp guards may be acceptable, but should be kept away
from window curtains.

Incense: Incense is not allowed to be burned on campus, including the residence halls.

Keys: At the beginning of the semester, at check-in, each student will be issued a key(s)/key card that
will open their room and the exterior entrances to other residential buildings during “open” hours. All
students must return their keys at the end of each term. Failure to do so will result in a fee for every key
not turned in. Keys may not be duplicated. See below (“Lost”) for lost key/card charges.

Laundry: Clothes washers and dryers are located in the basement of most residential facilities.
Problems with the equipment should be reported directly to the Wash company at 1-424-757-9195, or
through their website, wash.com. Please note that all maintenance by the company will occur during
Facilities office hours. There is no cost to the use of the washers and dryers.

Lock-Out Policy: Residents are expected to carry room and building keys (and IDs) with them at all
times. Locked doors and attention to keys are essential to students’ personal safety and the safety of
their property. In the event a student is locked out of their room during a college work day, the student
should call the Residence Life Office (608-363-2350). If it is after office hours or on the weekend, the
student should contact Security, who can let them in. If the student loses their key, they should contact
Residence Life immediately, fill out a key replacement form, and take it to Security to have a new key cut
for their room.

Lost ID/Meal Cards/Keys/Key Card: Students who have lost their ID card/meal card/key card
should report it to Residence Life and fill out a lost ID card form. Residence Life will make you a new
card for free if it is your first time losing it. Additional cards will have an associated cost to the student.
For lost room keys, house keys, and suite keys, there will be a replacement charge (and note that all
roommates will need new keys but only the student who lost the key will be charged).

If a student wants to dine on campus and does not have their ID/meal card, the student must pay the
guest meal price (with credit, food service venues are cashless). However, the student will be issued a
receipt and can receive a refund, in full, if the student presents the receipt and their valid ID at the Food
Service Office within 72 hours. ID cards are considered a part of your College record. Falsification or
tampering with College records (ID/meal card/key card) is considered a serious offense. Violators are
subject to disciplinary action, a fee, and possible loss of ID/meal card/key card.

Lost keys or IDs are to be taken to the Residence Life Office (if open) or Security and they will try to
track down the owner to return it.

Lounges and Public Areas: Lounges are provided for the use of residents and their guests but are not
to be used for the accommodation of overnight guests. Students are responsible collectively for the
condition of lounges and common areas, such as bathrooms and kitchens, in their part of the building.
The costs for repairing any damages to these common areas that cannot be assigned to a specific
individual will be shared by all residents of that area of the building. For safety reasons, residents or their
guests may not sleep overnight in the lounge areas.

Missing Residents:The College is obligated by law to endeavor to find residential students who are
missing. If you must leave campus (even for 24 hours), make sure that your RA, or advisor, or other
college official knows your whereabouts. See the complete policy.

Motorized Vehicles: No motorcycles or other motorized vehicles may be brought into the residence
halls.

Noise/Quiet Hours/Sound Equipment Policy: The residents of all residence halls have the
responsibility to be considerate of others at all times (24 hour courtesy hours). This means respecting
another’s right to sleep, study and not be disturbed. Each resident has the responsibility to confront any
noise problem that is of concern to them and to call upon a RA if assistance is needed. Each floor will
decide on guidelines to help maintain an atmosphere that will respect the rights of those residents who
are studying or sleeping. No stereos or sound equipment should be audible outside one’s room.
Residence Life reserves the right to confiscate sound amplification equipment for repeated or flagrant
violations of the noise policy. Again, out of consideration for others we require all residents to observe
“Quiet Hours.”

Quiet Hours:

● Sunday-Thursday 10:00 pm-8:00 am
● Friday & Saturday 12:00 am-10:00 am
● During Finals week there are 24-hour quiet hours that are enforced starting on the day
before the first day of Finals

Sound Equipment: Residents are always expected to keep sound equipment at a level
conducive to respecting the rights of others in a community. Stereo speakers must be kept out
of windows, and not played in a manner disturbing to the college or wider community. This
includes Greek houses, special interest houses, and all residential spaces (including outdoor
locations such as the Gazebo). All residents are encouraged to use headphones. Students are
not to use their rooms for practicing musical instruments, particularly those that may disturb
others.

Outdoor Areas Use: Use of the housing adjacent outdoor spaces (Aldrich Field, Chapin Quad, and the
609 Quad) for large-scale events, for example: music performances, inflatables, or temporary structure,
must be approved by the Director of Residential Life. Persons planning such events must also conform
to the Program, Activity, and Event Planning and the Social Host policies.

Overnight Guests: Residents may have overnight guests for up to three (3) consecutive nights. As
host, you need permission from your roommate, and a guest pass from Security. You also need to notify
your RA. All guests must comply with College regulations. Hosts are responsible for the conduct and
behavior of their guests. (See Guests of Students)

Pets: No pets, other than fish in an aquarium (5-gallon tank, maximum), are allowed to live on
campus with students. Residents having pets that live with them on campus may be required to have
the pet removed from campus and/or be required to move off-campus (and charged the full room rate
for the remainder of the semester as a result of not following this regulation). Residents who have pets
living with them may also be charged a daily fine, a cleaning charge (to be determined by Facilities ) for
each area damaged by the animal, and may be billed for the replacement of flooring in areas damaged
by their pet.

At times, off-campus people from the community, or families of students, will bring pets to campus.
Those pets are not allowed in any campus building, nor on the grounds of the Stadium area, and waste
must be picked up after them in the outdoor venues. Any charges for damages or cleaning are the
responsibility of the pet owner, or the student , if the student is found responsible for the presence of
the pet.

Note: Emotional Support Animals (as verified by LEADS) and service animals are not considered pets.

Privacy: A student has the right to the use of their room for sleep, study, and recreation. While the
right to sleep and study takes precedence over the right to recreation, roommates and suitemates are
expected to cooperate with each other in the use of their room(s). A student has the right not to be
affronted in their room by unreasonable noise originating there or elsewhere, or by behavior which
adversely affects the pursuit of their education. Students should not be subjected to conditions which
might involve them in a violation of the law. Each resident is responsible for the protection of their own
personal rights as well as those of others. This includes responsibility for confronting those who abuse
your rights. The RA will assist if needed but should not be expected to be the sole guardian of personal
rights. If practical, the College will not enter a student room unless accompanied by the student, their
authorized representative, or a second authorized College representative. The College reserves the
right to enter student rooms for the purpose of inspecting the premises when an authorized agent of
the College has reason to believe that:

1. an occupant of the room may be physically harmed or endangered;
2. damage is being done to the room;
3. College policy, as stated in the Beloit College catalog, Student Handbook, or subsequent
written notices is being violated;
4. housekeeping, room inspection, maintenance, and/or repair is necessary.

Due to the limitations of work schedules, and in an effort to utilize Facilities staff most efficiently,
workers may enter student rooms without prior notice to make necessary repairs. Workers will first
knock on the door, and if the student is present, explain the nature of the visit. If no one is present, the
workers will enter the room, make the necessary repairs and leave when these repairs are completed,
leaving the door locked with a note indicating that they were in the room. Students do not have the right
to deny access to their rooms to Facilities staff who are assigned to make necessary repairs.

Residence Hall Contracts (Housing Agreement form)/Refunds: The Housing Agreement form
is a binding, academic year-long agreement. It is a contract, and as such all students are encouraged to
read it carefully. Students who leave mid-semester are subject to the College Refund Policy found at:
https://www.beloit.edu/offices/bursar-tuition/policies/

Residence Life Coordinators (RLCs): Full-time professional staff who have been trained to
supervise student staff (R.A.s), manage the residential facilities, adjudicate policy violations, and provide
information counseling/support to students. All are part of the on-call team.

Resident Assistants (RAs): Each residence hall is staffed with at least one Resident Assistant (RA), an
upper-class student who has been carefully selected to act as a peer counselor, educator, social
programmer, advisor, and resource for their residents. As members and leaders within the residential
community, RAs have a responsibility to assist floor members in developing and maintaining an active,
respectful, and cooperative atmosphere. In short, they are concerned with the personal, social,
academic, and physical well-being of the students in the residence areas.

Room Alterations/Painting/Wall Hangings/Waterbeds: Alterations of the facilities,
including but not limited to, painting, building partitions, and removal of doors, are not permitted
without the written permission of the director of Residence Life and Facilities. Students will be
required to remove decorations or furnishings which are deemed to be a fire hazard.
Construction, decoration or arrangement of furnishings that hinders direct egress from the room
is not permitted. Students agree to hold harmless the board of trustees, the College, its officers,
employees and agents, and to indemnify them from any claims for damages sustained by the
student or others in the student’s assigned room as a result of the student’s acts or omissions
relating to any changes or modifications made by the student to the room or furnishings, such as
the construction of partitions or other structures. This clause makes the student financially
responsible to the College and releases the College in the event a person who is injured by a
hazard created or constructed by the student claims the College is liable for damages. Students
are required to return their room to the original condition by the checkout date or a fee will be
charged to the student.

Room Painting: Residence hall rooms are painted by Facilities painters. Any student who feels their room
needs painting should contact the Office of Residence Life. After an inspection of the room, if it is judged
that the room needs to be painted, Residence Life will schedule a painting time with the staff at Facilities.
No room may be painted more than once every three years.

Wall Hangings: No nails, tacks, screws, hooks, or pins may be driven into the surfaces of the room or
furniture. Pictures and other decorations may be mounted on the walls with artist putty, picture tape
or hooks that will not damage the paint on the walls. Students may be charged for damages to walls
in their room.

Water Beds: Due to the stress which they place on the floors, and the potential damage from leaks,
waterbeds are not permitted in the residence halls.

Room Assignments: Fall term room assignments for new students are made by the Office of
Residence Life, and are based as closely as possible on students’ expressed housing preferences.
All other students choose their own rooms based on credit hours during the preceding spring
term. Spring term room assignments are made as space becomes available.

Room Belongings: The College is not liable for theft, loss of, or damage to student personal
property housed in College facilities. Students are strongly encouraged to purchase renter’s
insurance for the coverage of their property.

Room Cancellations: As the Housing Agreement states, a student must inform the Office of
Residence Life in writing (reslife@beloit.edu) of their decision not to return to Beloit College.
[Students are also to complete an Exit Interview in the Dean of Students Office.] All students who
are leaving/not planning to live on campus, for whatever reason, must notify the Office of
Residence Life before July 1st for the following Fall semester and Dec. 1st for the Spring semester.

This includes leaves of absence (vacation term), withdrawals, graduation, study off-campus or
abroad, and moving off campus (when the residency requirement has been met). Failure to inform
the Office of Residence Life in writing by the deadlines indicated will result in a cancellation fee of
20 percent of one term’s rate. Moving/living off campus without meeting the stated criteria most
often results in being required to pay the College full room and board and being subject to
additional sanctions.

Room Changes/Switching Rooms/Changing Roommates: Any room changes must be
approved by the Residence Life Office. For roommate or floor mate conflicts, the process begins by
speaking with your roommate or floor mate. If it cannot be worked out, you must then speak with
your Resident Assistant (RA) to facilitate mediation. If resolution is still not made, then you are to
speak with your Residence Life Coordinator (RLC). If the situation still isn’t resolved, then a room
change may occur if there is space available. There are times where an immediate room change
may need to happen for various reasons; those are determined by the Office of Residence Life or
the Dean of Students.

Room Inventory and Damages: At the beginning of a student’s occupancy of a room, an inventory
of the condition of the room and its furnishings will be made by the student and the resident assistant.
Blank inventory forms are available through the RA and/or the Residence LifeOffice. Within the first
week of occupancy, students are to inventory the condition of their room in detail, being very specific
regarding any damages present upon moving into the room. Upon checkout, the room condition will be
compared to the initial inventory. Charges for damage done to the room and/or missing or broken
furniture will be assessed. Those who do not submit an initial inventory should know that the room will
be expected to be in perfect condition upon check-out, and there is no opportunity to appeal charges
without an initial inventory. Failure to complete and return a room inventory when moving into the
room will result in a charge to the student.

Room Personalization: Students are strongly encouraged to personalize their rooms. This can be
accomplished in several ways:

1. Moving and arranging the furniture to fit their style and living needs. (All furniture is kept
free standing and moveable for this reason.)
2. Decorate the walls with posters, pictures, etc.
3. Bring plants in to add color and a sense of home to the room.

Remember to keep in mind the policies under “Room Alterations” above.

Safety and Security: Every attempt is made to provide safe, secure buildings. All members of the
residence community must share responsibility for security. All residents can help in this process by:
reporting suspicious strangers to Security, keeping fire doors closed, not propping open outer doors
when they are locked, and keeping room doors locked. The College cannot accept responsibility for

damage, theft, or loss of personal property. Students are encouraged to obtain insurance on their
possessions. Students must never hand out their key or ID to another person for them to use.

All members of the residential community must share responsibility for security. Residents are issued a
key or key card for the exterior doors to their building. Key cards normally allow access from 10:00 a.m.
– 10:00 p.m. After that time, only the building assignment card will open the doors. These hours are
subject to change.

Room Safety: When someone knocks on your door, ask who it is and check the person, using
the door viewer provided, before responding. You should keep your room door locked at all
times. Each student is encouraged to record the serial numbers of all valuable items. It is also
advisable to make special arrangements for the protection of valuables when absent for
extended periods. The protection of personal property is the individual’s responsibility.

For additional information and policies regarding safety and security go here.

Smoking/Vaping: Smoking and vaping are not allowed in any building on campus, including
residence halls. See the Smoking/Vaping policy for additional details.

Solicitation: Salespersons are prohibited from selling in residence halls and from door-to-door
solicitation unless they have written authorization from the Director of Residential Life. No one is to
place flyers on cars or mark cars parked on campus in any way.

Space Heaters: Space heaters are not allowed in residence spaces due to the fire danger they
present. If your room is too cold please fill out a work order (Asset Essentials) request via the Portal or
contact your RA for assistance.

Storage: Storage areas for student belongings are available only in Whitney and Porter Hall and
for those students that are either international students, live more than 1,000 miles away, or are
going abroad for the semester. Storage is on a first-come, first-served basis. Students are allowed
to store up to five bins and all must be clearly labeled with their name, class year and box number.

Vending Machines: Vending machines are conveniently located in several residence halls.
Additionally, more machines are available in the Powerhouse,the lower level of Pearsons Hall, and on
the first floor of Morse Library Problems with the equipment, including refunds, should be reported
using the contact information listed on the machine.

Weapons and Explosives: The use and/or storage of any weapons, firearms, ammunition, or
explosives of any kind in the residence halls are strictly prohibited. Laboratory chemicals and fireworks
also are not permitted in the residence halls. (See: Weapons.)

3. Continuing Student Enrollment, Housing, and Accounting Holds

Payment Responsibilities
Once a student is enrolled at Beloit College, the student is responsible for paying all tuition, fees, and other
charges assessed to their student account. All charges added to a student’s account before the semester
payment deadline must be paid by the semester payment deadline published on the Beloit College website.
Charges assessed after the semester due date must be paid by the date specified on the student's billing
statement or other notice from the College.

Accounting Holds
If payment is not received by the specified due date, or payments are not made per the approved payment
plan agreement, an Accounting/Enrollment hold will be placed on the student’s account.

All students must resolve Accounting/Enrollment holds before they may:

● Move into the Residence Halls
● Participate in the room placement for the following academic year
● Register for courses
● Attend classes
● Participate in any co-curricular activities
