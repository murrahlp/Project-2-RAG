Resolving Accounting Holds
A student may resolve an Accounting/Enrollment hold by contacting the Student Accounts Office to determine
the nature of the hold and the actions required to address it. Required actions may include, but are not limited
to:
● Paying the outstanding balance in full
● Securing sufficient outside funding to pay the outstanding balance in full
● Establishing an approved payment plan
● Completing outstanding financial documents (if applicable)

Students Already Living on Campus
Students who are already living in the Residence Halls due to summer housing or co-curricular activities must
clear an Accounting/Enrollment hold no later than one week before the first day of classes. Students who do
not meet this deadline will be required to move out of the Residence Halls and may not attend classes or
participate in co-curricular activities.

In some cases, the Student Accounts Office may provide written authorization for a student to live or remain in
campus housing while following an approved action plan. Participation in co-curricular activities may be
approved in the action plan, depending on the unique situation. The student must receive this permission
before moving into or continuing to live in the Residence Halls.

International Students
Failure to enroll in classes may negatively impact international students’ status in the United States. For more
information on how this affects their status, please contact the International Student Advisor.

Other Enrollment Holds
Students may have other enrollment holds for reasons unrelated to an outstanding balance. Students with
these holds must contact the Dean of Students Office and develop an action plan based on the nature of the
hold.

H. Mail Center
The mail center serves two primary functions: (1) as a central mail distribution center for the
U.S./International mail and packages delivered to campus. As such, regular postal rules apply to all
stamped mail. Any questionable stamped mail will follow the U.S. postal procedures for follow up
inquiry; (2) as a College service for inter-campus mail. Incidents reported to mail center staff or by mail
center staff will follow the normal campus investigation/disciplinary process.

The Campus Mail Center also retains the right to add additional policies and procedures, given the
nature of our on-campus facility and staffing. Students are to make sure that mail and packages include
their proper name and their student box number to ensure that their mail and packages are delivered in
a timely manner and to the correct person.

Relevant Campus Policies and Procedures

Handling of Questionable Mail:
Mail received at the Mail Center which is sealed and properly addressed will be distributed to the
appropriate mailbox(es) as long as there is no outward indication of any inappropriateness.
Examples of inappropriateness include but are not limited to: obvious food/candy in the envelope,
hurtful/hateful message on the envelope or in the address, graphic nudity on the outside of the
envelope.

Mail which arrives at the Mail Center which can be readily seen (not sealed in envelope) by Mail Center
personnel to contain questionable content will be referred to the Dean of Students, Student
Engagement and Leadership or Residence Life Office for approval/disapproval prior to distribution. If a
mail patron receives what they consider offensive or solicitous mail and a complaint is made to the
department, any similarly appearing pieces will be delayed in processing pending a decision on
appropriateness from the Student Engagement and Leadership or Residence Life Office.

Items which are reviewed and deemed inappropriate will be returned to the sender if the sender is
known and the return is approved. Inappropriate items with no known sender will be discarded. Candy
and foodstuffs of any type in campus mail are prohibited by health code. Should these items be
discovered, either through outward appearance, or through information that similar envelopes or
packages are being sent, the items will be returned to the sender (if known) or discarded (if not known).

Misaddressed Mail Handling:
Campus mail which is addressed incorrectly for proper name and/or box number will be returned
to the sender if there is a return address. If there is no return address on the mail piece(s), the Mail
Center Manager will open the mail and attempt to identify the sender. If successful, the mail will be
returned along with a note indicating the mail piece(s) had to be opened for that purpose. If the
sender cannot be identified, the mail will be held for 10 days to give the sender time to claim the
mail. If no one has identified and claimed the mail in 10 days, it will be discarded. Should, in the
course of this process, questionable content be discovered, the mail will be directed to the Dean of
Students Office for review and a determination of its status.

Students on Leave of Absences (Vacation Term) or that Withdraw from the College:
Students who leave the college temporarily or permanently are to provide the Mail Center with an
address where their first-class mail may be forwarded. Students on leave or withdrawn are also to
remember to make sure to change their package deliveries to a non-college address. Students who
are on Vacation Term will maintain their same college mailbox (for when they return). Students
who withdraw will no longer have access to a college mailbox.

Campus Stuffings:
In the rare event that a student or student group wants to put paper mail flyers or envelopes in
individual mailboxes, they must first be approved by the Student Engagement & Leadership Office.
Generally, stuffings will be approved if they are deemed to be campus-life related and are not

offensive or solicitous in nature. Stuffings from political or religious groups, other non-College
organizations and/or businesses are typically not permitted in campus mail regardless of whether
or not a student or students are members of the group/organization. Exceptions must be
approved by both the Dean of Students and the Vice President for Finance and Planning.

I. Motor Vehicle, Parking, and Parking Permits
In order to park in College-owned/managed parking spaces, all motor vehicles must be registered with
Security (see parking policy below and https://www.beloit.edu/offices/security/campus-city-parking/).
Once registered, Security will make every attempt to notify students if their vehicle is involved in an
accident, found with its lights on, or any other legitimate need. Security will make a reasonable attempt
to notify the student before towing for illegal parking, unless the vehicle is parked in a tow-away zone
(fire lane, reserved space, disabled stall, etc.). Students are to observe signage regarding restricted
parking, including “faculty/staff only” parking sections, “residence life staff” parking spaces, and “20
minute parking” spots. Failure to comply may result in tickets and, if necessary, towing the car.

Vehicles parked illegally (including parking on campus without a permit) will be ticketed by Security.
There is a fine for general violations and double the fine for parking in handicapped spaces. Tickets
must be paid within 30 days and may be paid in the Security Office. After 30 days, the fine will double
and the charge will be added to the student account. NOTE: If a permit is purchased at the time a fine
is incurred, the fine is waived. This is subject to permit availability.

Motorized vehicles may not be taken into residence halls and may not be operated on sidewalks or
grass areas. Motorcycles may be pushed on sidewalks or paths to put them in sheltered or secured
areas. Motorcycles are not to be parked under the Blaisdell/Whitney covered walkways.

Students may only park only in areas designated by Campus Security as Student Parking if they have
purchased a parking permit. This includes the off-street parking spaces in front of Whitney, Blaisdell,
and Bushnell Halls; along College Street from Emerson to Clary Streets; the parking areas behind 811
College Street; parking west of FemCo, east of Morse Library, the Powerhouse lot, and the Aldrich
Parking lot. Students should also observe “no overnight parking” signs.

Any vehicle parked in a fire lane or on the grass will be subject to towing at the owner’s expense without
notice. [Except at the Stadium during athletic events when directed to do so.] Vehicles parked in
undesignated or otherwise inappropriate areas may be ticketed by Security. If a significant number of
tickets are issued to a particular vehicle or owner, their vehicle may be towed without notice and at the
student’s expense. Students should also be aware that city ordinance gives the Beloit Police the
authority to ticket and/or tow vehicles illegally parked in handicap parking areas (on campus and off).
