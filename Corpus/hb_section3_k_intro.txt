K. Safety and Security
Student cooperation with campus security is required to establish an environment which allows
safe and unencumbered movement of persons on campus while maintaining a high degree of
access to College grounds and facilities for townspeople and other guests of the College
community. Action clearly intended to violate such security policies as may be instituted will result
in judicial action.

The College construes performance of the following acts as constituting violation or attempted
violation of the security policy and is grounds for judicial action:
1. Destroying locks on campus buildings.
2. Willful harassment of security officers.
3. Removal, destruction, or defacement of posted signs.
4. Failure to show a College ID card or to obtain one when requested by a member of the
College faculty or staff, particularly security staff acting in the performance of their duties.
5. Personal harassment, physical or verbal abuse against any officer during the performance of
their duties.

The responsibilities of College security staff are:
1. The protection of persons.
2. The protection of property.
3. The protection of the integrity of the College.

The Security officers have the authority to request all persons on College property to show identification
cards or passes. In addition, because of their special role at the College, Security officers are forbidden
from having amorous, romantic, or sexual relationships with Beloit College students.

Beloit College is a private institution, and all College-owned or controlled property is private property.
The College may designate certain buildings, facilities, or outdoor areas as open to the public for specific
events or purposes; however, such designation does not convert the property into public property or
create a general right of public access.

College buildings are closed to individuals who are not current students, employees, contractors,
trustees, or authorized guests, except as otherwise designated by the College. Members of the public
who are not authorized or invited to be on College property or grounds may be directed to leave at any
time. This includes individuals seeking access for personal, professional, governmental, or other
purposes including law enforcement personnel who do not have authorization or lawful authority to be
present on College property. Individuals who refuse to leave when asked may be trespassed and subject
to local laws..

1. Emergency Notifications & Community Alerts (Timely Warnings)

The Safety and Security Office is the primary office which sends “Rave Alerts,” also known as “Emergency
Notifications,” via the Rave Alert mass notification system. We utilize email, text, and phone calls to alert
our campus about an active and immediate threat to the life safety of campus. The College uses the
CLEAR framework when determining whether an Emergency Notification should be issued.

● C – Confirmed
● L - Life-threatening
● E - Emergency
● A - Active (or Imminent)
● R - Reportable Geography (on our campus or public property directly adjacent to it) at large.

The Safety and Security Director, Emergency Operations Center Manager, Dean of Students, Human
Resources Director, and other Senior Leadership may be involved with the decision to send a Rave Alert if
there is ample time to consider whether the threat is truly CLEAR. We will only send out an “ALL-CLEAR”
alert if we already sent a Rave Alert, and only once it is reasonably safe to resume normal activities.

The College also issues Community Alerts, also known as Timely Warnings, through email
communications using the [Safety] tag. These notifications are intended to inform the College community
about Clery-reportable crimes or other safety concerns that represent an ongoing or continuing threat
within the College’s Clery Geography, even when the threat is not considered an immediate or imminent
danger to life.

Not all crimes or hazardous incidents will result in a Timely Warning. Timely Warnings are issued when
they are determined to be appropriate and likely to assist in protecting the campus community or
preventing similar incidents. Decisions regarding whether to issue a Timely Warning are made on a
case-by-case basis and typically involve the Director of Safety and Security in consultation with
appropriate College officials, such as the Dean of Students or Human Resources Director.
